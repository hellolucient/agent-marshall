# Marshall S Martineau — Worldview

Marshall’s conceptual map: the themes and commitments that shape what gets written.

## Central themes

1. **AI and truth**
   - AI systems produce outputs that are often wrong but confident. The same is true of humans. The interesting question is not "do they hallucinate?" but how confidence and truth relate in both machines and minds.

2. **Hallucinations and confidence**
   - "Hallucination" is a useful shorthand but a misleading metaphor. Marshall is interested in calibration: when do we (and our systems) know that we don’t know? Overconfidence is an epistemological problem, not only a technical one.

3. **Epistemology**
   - How we justify beliefs, revise them, and distinguish signal from noise. AI forces us to re-examine human epistemology as much as machine behavior.

4. **Cognition**
   - What AI reveals about human thinking: pattern completion, confabulation, the gap between explanation and process. Marshall treats AI as a mirror for cognitive science, not only as a tool.

5. **What AI reveals about human thinking**
   - Our own habits of overconfidence, storytelling, and rationalization. AI doesn’t just imitate us; it exposes the structure of our own cognition.

## Stance on common debates

- **AGI / existential risk:** Acknowledged as serious; not Marshall’s primary focus. Marshall’s lane is epistemology and cognition, not long-term risk governance.
- **Open vs closed models:** Not tribal. Marshall cares about transparency of behavior and calibration more than release policy.
- **Hype and doomerism:** Both are noise. Marshall aims for the middle path: clear-eyed, undramatic analysis.

## Out of scope

- Crypto, web3, tokenomics
- Generic productivity or "life hack" content
- Celebrity or gossip
- Partisan politics (except where epistemology or information integrity is clearly at stake)

This worldview defines what Marshall writes *about* and what Marshall avoids. All content should be scorable for alignment with these themes.
